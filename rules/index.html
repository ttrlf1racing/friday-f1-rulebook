Article 01

League Conduct

The standards expected of every championship participant.

1.1 Sporting behaviour

Drivers must compete fairly, honestly and in a manner that protects the reputation of the championship. Deliberate contact, retaliation, harassment, discriminatory conduct and attempts to manipulate results are prohibited.

1.2 Communications

League channels must remain constructive. Disagreement is permitted; personal attacks, public steward-bashing and abusive voice or text communications are not.

1.3 Responsibility

Entry in an official session confirms that the driver has read and accepted the current published regulations.

Article 02

Driver Registration & Attendance

Eligibility, sign-up and absence requirements.

2.1 Registration

Each driver must register with one consistent platform identity and racing number. Account sharing or racing under another entrant’s identity is prohibited.

2.2 Attendance

Drivers must confirm attendance by the published deadline. A confirmed driver who cannot race must notify league administration as soon as reasonably possible.

2.3 Reserves

Reserve seats are assigned by administration. A reserve assumes the sporting obligations of the seat for that event but does not inherit another driver’s disciplinary record.

Article 03

Race Weekend Format

The official sequence from lobby opening to classification.

3.1 Event sequence

Unless a bulletin states otherwise, an event consists of lobby checks, qualifying and the race. Administration will publish session length, weather policy and start time before the event.

3.2 Joining

Drivers must be present and ready before the lobby deadline. Joining, spectating or retiring in a way that disrupts a live qualifying lap is prohibited.

3.3 Restarts

A restart may be ordered only by league administration for a material technical failure. Individual disconnections or first-lap incidents do not automatically justify a restart.

Article 04

Driving Standards

Predictable, controlled and respectful racing at all times.

4.1 Car control

A driver must remain in control and leave a reasonable margin for foreseeable changes in speed or direction. Loss of control does not remove responsibility for resulting contact.

4.2 Rejoining

A car leaving the circuit may rejoin only when safe and without gaining a lasting advantage. The rejoining driver must yield to cars already on the racing surface.

4.3 Damage and recovery

A damaged or spun car must act predictably. Holding the brakes after a spin and moving off-line when safe are expected where they reduce danger.

Article 05

Track Limits

Use of the circuit and the treatment of gained advantages.

5.1 Circuit boundary

The white lines define the track unless event notes specify otherwise. Drivers must make every reasonable effort to remain within them.

5.2 Advantage gained

A driver who gains time or position by leaving the track must surrender that advantage safely. Returning a position does not prevent review where another car was endangered.

5.3 Repeated breaches

Game-issued warnings and penalties remain valid unless stewards find clear evidence of a game error. Systematic exploitation may receive an additional sporting penalty.

Article 06

Overtaking

Rights and responsibilities during an on-track battle.

6.1 Attacking car

An overtaking attempt must be sufficiently controlled to complete the corner without avoidable contact. A late dive that depends on the other driver taking evasive action may be penalised.

6.2 Racing room

Where meaningful overlap exists at the relevant point, both drivers must leave reasonable racing room. The exact obligation depends on corner geometry, car position and control.

6.3 Defending

One defensive move is permitted on a straight. Moving in reaction, crowding beyond the track edge or making multiple direction changes is prohibited.

Article 07

Pit Lane

Safe entry, exit and operation in the pit lane.

7.1 Entry and exit

Drivers must respect pit entry and exit lines and avoid sudden moves that endanger following traffic. A car leaving the pits must not force a car on track to take evasive action.

7.2 Pit speed

The limiter and applicable speed limit must be observed. Game-issued speeding penalties normally stand.

7.3 Unsafe release

Drivers retain responsibility for awareness when released by the game. Contact or obstruction caused during release may be reviewed.

Article 08

Safety Car

Conduct during neutralised race conditions.

8.1 Delta and spacing

Drivers must obey the game delta, avoid erratic acceleration or braking, and maintain a safe distance. Deliberately creating excessive gaps is prohibited.

8.2 Overtaking

Overtaking under safety car or virtual safety car conditions is prohibited except where the game directs it or a car is clearly unable to maintain pace.

8.3 Restart

The leader controls the pace within game limits and must act predictably. Drivers may not create overlap or complete an overtake before the prescribed restart point.

Article 09

Incident Reporting

How incidents are submitted and assessed by the stewards.

9.1 Submission window

Reports must be submitted through the official channel within the published deadline and identify the lap, drivers involved and alleged breach.

9.2 Evidence

Video should show sufficient time before and after the incident and, where possible, include multiple viewpoints. Edited evidence that removes relevant context may be rejected.

9.3 Steward review

Stewards assess available evidence, consequences, driver control and relevant precedent. Parties must not contact stewards privately to influence a decision.

Article 10

Steward Penalties

Proportionate sanctions for sporting infringements.

10.1 Available penalties

Stewards may issue a warning, licence points, time penalty, position adjustment, qualifying restriction, pit-lane start, race suspension or championship exclusion.

10.2 Assessment

Severity, avoidability, consequence, cooperation and relevant history may affect the penalty. A more serious outcome does not automatically prove a more serious breach.

10.3 Appeals

An appeal must identify a material procedural error or significant new evidence unavailable during the original review. Disagreement with steward judgment alone is not grounds for appeal. Administration’s appeal decision is final.

Article 11

Championship Points

Classification, scoring and tie-break procedures.

11.1 Classification

Points are awarded according to the championship’s published scoring table after steward decisions have been applied. A driver must be officially classified to score unless stated otherwise.

11.2 Dead heats

Ties in the standings are resolved by most wins, then second places and subsequent finishing positions, followed by the best most-recent result.

11.3 Adjustments

Administration may correct clerical or calculation errors. Sporting outcomes are amended only through the stewarding and appeals process.

Article 12

Lobby Settings

The controlled configuration for official sessions.

12.1 Published configuration

Assists, damage, weather, parc fermé, safety car and session settings are defined in the event bulletin or championship settings schedule.

12.2 Verification

Drivers must raise an incorrect lobby setting before the session begins. Continuing to compete may be treated as acceptance where the setting does not materially compromise fairness.

12.3 Connection quality

A persistently unstable connection that endangers others may result in removal from a session until reasonable stability is demonstrated.

Article 13

Respect for Officials

Constructive engagement with administrators and stewards.

13.1 Decisions

Drivers may request clarification or use the appeal process respectfully. Abuse, intimidation or coordinated pressure directed at an official is prohibited.

13.2 Confidentiality

Confidential steward discussions, private reports and personal information must not be published without authorisation.

13.3 Cooperation

Participants must provide truthful information when requested and comply with reasonable instructions needed to run an official event.

Article 14

League Administration

Authority and operational responsibilities of the league.

14.1 Sporting authority

Administration interprets these regulations, appoints officials, publishes calendars and manages entries in the interests of safe and fair competition.

14.2 Bulletins

An official bulletin may clarify or amend regulations. Material changes will state an effective date and be communicated through an official league channel.

14.3 Exceptional circumstances

Where circumstances are not contemplated by the rules, administration may issue a reasoned decision consistent with fairness and the intent of the championship.

Article 15

General Regulations

Final provisions governing the championship rulebook.

15.1 Precedence

Event-specific bulletins take precedence for that event. The latest published version of these regulations replaces all earlier versions.

15.2 Interpretation

Headings aid navigation but do not limit a rule’s meaning. References to a driver include their entrant or representative where appropriate.

15.3 Acceptance

Participation constitutes acceptance of the regulations, official decisions and the championship’s reasonable use of race footage and results for league operations.
